A host that governs an organization can read what that organization knows about a native identity: `POST /v1/host/organizations/:id/identities/lookup` with `{ identityId }` (the host session token's `sub`) answers the identity's status, its verified email (or null), its active login binding `{ id, revision }` (or null), its memberships in the organization `[{ id, status, role, authorizationRevision, sharedWorkspaceAccess }]` (any status answers; the host must refuse a membership that is not `active`) and the organization's invitations it accepted. Every answer is `cache-control: no-store`. It authenticates like the host commands (service delegated token for the organization with `members:manage`, an administrator subject, a governed organization) and writes nothing. An identity with no membership in the organization answers `404 HOST_IDENTITY_NOT_IN_ORGANIZATION`, the same as an unknown one. New contracts `HostLookupOrganizationIdentityRequest` and `HostOrganizationIdentity` in `@opengeni/contracts/organization-governance`. Without the governance port the route does not exist.

Host commands whose actor the database refuses (the token's subject is not an active owner or admin, or a role rule) keep their `403` and message and now carry `details.code = "HOST_COMMAND_ACTOR_NOT_AUTHORIZED"`.

Migration 1016 (rolling) adds `organization_membership_invitations.accepted_at`, set by trigger when an invitation is accepted and filled for invitations accepted before it, and `opengeni_private.lookup_organization_host_identity(uuid, text, uuid)`, the lookup by identity. Its runtime `EXECUTE` is withheld for one release (`DEFERRED_RUNTIME_PRIVATE_ROUTINES`; tests grant it with `grantDeferredRuntimeAccess`); until a later migration grants it, the route answers the same through the organization's member list.

The host's invitation revoke command now says why it refused: `409` with `details.code = "HOST_INVITATION_REVISION_CONFLICT"` (and `details.status = "pending"`) when the invitation is still pending at another revision, or `"HOST_INVITATION_NOT_PENDING"` with `details.status` `"accepted"`, `"revoked"` or `"expired"` (`accepted` and `revoked` are final; `expired` can no longer be accepted, and a revoke at its current revision still records it as revoked). Every other answer is unchanged, and replaying the same `operationId` after success still returns the original receipt. A host can read one of its organization's invitations back with `GET /v1/host/organizations/:id/invitations/:invitationId` (`HostOrganizationInvitation`: `id`, `organizationId`, `status`, `revision`, `acceptedMembershipId`, `acceptedAt`, `expiresAt`). It authenticates like the identity lookup, writes nothing, and sends `cache-control: no-store` on every answer. An invitation that is unknown or belongs to another organization answers `404 HOST_INVITATION_NOT_IN_ORGANIZATION`. New constants `HOST_INVITATION_NOT_IN_ORGANIZATION`, `HOST_INVITATION_REVISION_CONFLICT` and `HOST_INVITATION_NOT_PENDING`.

Each membership in the identity lookup carries the fences of the host's member commands: `authorizationRevision` (the `expectedAuthorizationRevision` of `members/:id/change-role`, `suspend`, `reactivate` and `offboard`) and `sharedWorkspaceAccess: [{ workspaceId, membershipId, role, permissions, updatedAt }]`, the membership's grants in the organization's shared workspaces ordered by workspace name. `updatedAt` is the `expectedUpdatedAt` of `PUT` and `DELETE workspaces/:ws/members/:id` at the database's full (microsecond) precision; send it back exactly as received. Migration 1017 replaces the withheld lookup routine's body with these fields, and the member-list fallback answers the same through the organization administration overview.

`bootstrapWorkspace` takes a new opt-in flag, `governedOrganizationOwner`. With it, a call that creates the organization (decided by the account insert itself, so concurrent first bootstraps write one owner) also makes the subject the organization's active owner, so host commands and reads signed for that subject are authorized: it writes an `organization_memberships` owner row, the hidden Personal workspace every active membership has (no access to it) and that workspace's `workspace_inference_controls` row. It never does so for the `opengeni:local`, `opengeni:configured` and `better-auth:user` accounts, or for identity-backed subjects (`user:`, `external_user:`). Without the flag (the default) `bootstrapWorkspace` writes no organization membership, as before. For an organization bootstrapped earlier, `POST /v1/host/organizations/:id/host-owner/ensure` with `{ actingExternalUser, command: { operationId } }` makes the service token's own subject the owner of the token's own organization, once: the subject must own one of the organization's workspaces, identity-backed subjects are refused (`403 HOST_COMMAND_ACTOR_NOT_AUTHORIZED`), an existing membership of the subject that is not an active owner is `409 HOST_ORGANIZATION_OWNER_CONFLICT` and is never changed, and other memberships are never touched. It answers `HostOrganizationOwner` (`{ membershipId, subjectId, created }`), is recorded in `organization_host_command_attributions` as `host_owner.ensure` (the database adds an owner to an existing organization only for such a recorded operation by the same subject), and requires a governed organization. Migration 1017 (rolling) creates `opengeni_private.ensure_organization_host_owner(uuid, text, uuid)` with its runtime `EXECUTE` withheld for one release; until a later migration grants it, `bootstrapWorkspace` writes no owner and the ensure answers `503 HOST_ORGANIZATION_OWNER_UNAVAILABLE` (retryable) without writing anything. New exports `HostEnsureOrganizationOwnerRequest`, `HostOrganizationOwner`, `HOST_ORGANIZATION_OWNER_UNAVAILABLE`, `HOST_ORGANIZATION_OWNER_CONFLICT` and `HOST_ORGANIZATION_OWNER_ENSURE` (`@opengeni/contracts/organization-governance`), and `ensureOrganizationHostOwner` and `hostOrganizationOwnerGranted` (`@opengeni/db`).
